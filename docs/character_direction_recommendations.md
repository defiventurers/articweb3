# Character Direction Recommendations for the Heritage Catalogue

## Design principle

These recommendations turn counters into **clear playable characters**, not generic mascots. Each character should be a single, readable silhouette at board scale, use a strong team colour and a unique prop or posture, and preserve the traditional game’s actual roles. Sacred figures, ethnic caricatures, military insignia, and costume claims without a source should be avoided. For card and sowing games, a friendly **steward, creature, or living-material character** is more apt than forcing a chess-piece treatment.

| Game | Verified source role | Recommended characters | Why this is apt |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nine Ice Forts** | Navakankari line-forming tribes | **Aurora fort scouts** versus **coral fort scouts**; compact hooded figures carrying a small banner and round shield | The game is about placing, linking, and immobilising. Scouts make the tactical line readable without inventing a specific historical army. |
| **Four-Wing Ice Hunt** | Cows-and-Leopards hunt | **Eight red colony penguins** versus **two improved snow leopards** | This preserves the game’s real asymmetry—many herd pieces against two hunters—and follows the already established penguin/leopard identity. |
| **Aurora Ganjifa Academy** | Mughal Ganjifa suit and trick play | **Suit heralds**: a moon-court messenger, sun-court messenger, crown keeper, and garden keeper; used as restrained card-corner characters | The game is card-led, so characters belong in the suit illustrations rather than replacing cards on a board. Heralds clarify suit identity and trick ownership. |
| **Aurora Vulture** | Kaooa: vulture versus crows | **One large Himalayan vulture** versus **seven alert crows** | These are the original animal roles. Distinct wingspan and flock silhouettes make the unequal sides instantly understandable. |
| **Break the Ice** | Panchi race runners | **Two groups of alpine courier children**, each with four compact runners and a cowrie pouch | A courier’s route, safe stop, and exact finish naturally fit a cowrie race. The character is non-specific and remains respectful of the game’s provenance. |
| **Cowrie Kingdoms** | Ashta-Kashte spiral race | **Eight court envoys** in two colour families, each with a small scroll or lamp | An envoy travels across a palace-like route and gives every runner a purpose without claiming a particular dynasty or costume. |
| **Crown Run** | Dadu race with a special royal piece | **Court runners** led by a distinct **crown bearer** | The crown bearer makes the special Nakta/royal role visible at a glance; supporting runners remain simpler so the rule hierarchy is legible. |
| **Fishflow** | Pallanguzhi relay sowing | **Two seed-sorting garden sprites** with tiny bowls; counters become smiling seeds only inside the pits | Pallanguzhi is fundamentally a sowing game. Garden stewards explain gathering and relaying, while seeds retain the quick counting readability players need. |
| **Forty Glacier Guards** | Chalis Gutiya field formation | **Two sets of four-season garrison guards**—icy blue and warm coral—with a simple spear-free lantern posture | The game’s formation and leaps feel like coordinated guard movement. Lanterns avoid aggressive militarism while making the two sides readable. |
| **Glacier Trail** | Pancha Keliya house race | **Five-house caravan travellers**—a small yak-freezing-route guide and a coral trail guide—each with matching companions | The named houses and travel route make a caravan story intuitive. The characters give “stored throws” and arrival timing a friendly journey metaphor. |
| **Ice Hunters** | Bagh-Chal: tigers and goats | **Two snow tigers** versus **twenty mountain goats** | The original predator–prey roles are already the most apt characters. Use crouching tiger and clustered goat silhouettes rather than human substitutes. |
| **Ice Rings** | Pretwa ring/circuit tactics | **Two bands of circular-route wardens**, each wearing a ring clasp and carrying a small route marker | The board’s rings and connected movement are the central idea. Wardens communicate guarding routes, not conquest, and their clasp makes them recognisable at token scale. |
| **Khasi Fishflow** | Mawkar Katiya sowing | **Two hill-garden keepers** with woven-basket motifs; physical stones remain as their harvest | The recommended character honours the farming/sowing metaphor without making unverified costume claims. Stones stay countable, while keepers animate the turn ownership. |
| **Polar Tablan** | Tablan stick-score race | **Two scorekeepers** with bundled counting sticks and a route satchel | Counting sticks are part of the gameplay language, so scorekeepers explain the cast-and-allocate loop better than unrelated warriors or animals. |
| **Ruma Ice Puzzle** | Tchuka Ruma fish sowing | **A river heron guide** and **a school of bright fish** | The original rule language already centres on fish and a Ruma. A quiet heron makes the teaching/puzzle framing gentle while fish remain the actual moving units. |
| **Seven Ice Rings** | Sat-gol ring flow | **Seven pebble spirits** in a single family, distinguished only by blue or coral glow | Sat-gol is a circular flow puzzle, not a battle. Tiny pebble spirits preserve rapid pit-counting and give the forced movement a playful personality. |
| **Sige** | Siga route race | **Two carved route guardians**—an aurora sun-bird and ember moon-deer—with a shared stepped base | Sige’s outer and inner route split needs unmistakable two-player icons. Decorative carved-animal figures are culturally neutral, easy to read, and fit a historic board-race material. |
| **Sixteen Ice Warriors** | Hewakam Keliya formation and jumps | **Two sixteen-member shield line squads**, aurora and ember, each represented by a helmeted but faceless sentry | The original game’s language and tactical pattern are martial. A simple, non-historical sentry silhouette supports formation and chained jumps without claiming a real uniform. |
| **Sky Temple Run** | Vimanam six-counter race | **Six temple pilgrims per side**, one aurora and one coral procession, each carrying a small travel lamp | The named Vimanam route, gate, bridge, and inner arms naturally support a pilgrimage journey. Lamps read well at small size and reinforce route progress. |
| **Two Stones** | Do Guti five-point duel | **Two tiny summit spirits**—a rounded ice sprite and a rounded coral sprite | The game is deliberately abstract and minimal. Two simple opposing characters preserve the five-point duel’s clarity without overloading the board. |

## Non-negotiable implementation rules

Each character must fit inside its existing hit target and never conceal a dot, rail, pit count, card rank, or legal-action halo. Team recognition must survive grayscale by combining colour with silhouette, prop, and pose. During a capture, use a brief departure or friendly retreat—not violence. Reuse only a visual family within a single game; do not use a penguin, tiger, or generic warrior as a catalogue-wide substitute.

> **Recommendation:** Begin with the three games where characters are already inherent to the rules—Four-Wing Ice Hunt, Aurora Vulture, and Ice Hunters—then move to Sky Temple Run, Ruma Ice Puzzle, and the two sowing games. These changes will feel the most natural while establishing the small-scale character language for the more abstract boards.

## Catalogue continuity

The individual character concepts now belong to a shared recurring cast: **Auri** (Aurora Scout), **Cori** (Coral Courier), **Mica** (Pebble Sprite), and **Pip** (Lantern Bird).
