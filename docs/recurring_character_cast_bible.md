# Arctic Dominion Recurring Character Cast Bible

## Core decision

The heritage catalogue should feel like a connected world, but it must not flatten distinct traditional games into one generic cast. The answer is a small **travelling cast** that changes jobs from game to game while keeping the same silhouette, colour cues, and personality. Source-critical animal roles—penguins and leopards, vulture and crows, tigers and goats—remain themselves. They can be accompanied by the recurring cast in cover art, rules, or the Field guide, but they should not be replaced as playable pieces.

## The four returning characters

| Character | Stable visual anchors | Personality and function | Safe recurrence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Auri, the Aurora Scout** | Teal hood, four-point ice clasp, round lantern-shield, soft triangular silhouette | Calm planner who helps players notice structure before acting | Aurora-side scout, pilgrim, envoy, route guardian, court runner, or learning guide |
| **Cori, the Coral Courier** | Coral coat, shell compass, warm circular satchel, rounded silhouette | Energetic route-finder who encourages a first try and a rematch | Coral-side courier, pilgrim, envoy, route guardian, court runner, or learning guide |
| **Mica, the Pebble Sprite** | Small smooth stone body, two bright eyes, blue/coral glow variants, no clothing | Quiet counter-keeper who makes abstract routes, seeds, stones, and rings feel alive | Seed, fish companion, stone, pebble spirit, score marker, or pit-side helper |
| **Pip, the Lantern Bird** | Tiny snowy bird, gold lantern bead, navy wing tips, arcing flight silhouette | A non-player guide who appears in rules, field-guide cards, and results states | Universal tutorial and celebration guide; never occupies a competitive board space |

> **Recognition rule:** Auri always carries the four-point clasp, Cori always carries the shell compass, Mica is always a polished pebble with a team glow, and Pip always has the lantern bead. Costumes and poses can change, but these anchors cannot.

## How recurrence should feel

The player should recognise a character before reading a label: Auri’s ice clasp, Cori’s compass, Mica’s polished glow, and Pip’s lantern bead are the catalogue’s visual handshake. Auri and Cori should never both be the same team; they are the recurring Aurora/Coral counterparts. Mica can be an actual counter only when a game already uses small stones, seeds, fish-adjacent pieces, or abstract tokens. Pip should stay outside competition—as a guide, not a third player.

## Production constraints

Characters must fit existing token hit areas and preserve contrast in grayscale. At board scale, use a face only if it remains readable; otherwise, silhouette plus prop is sufficient. Use no culturally specific costume, sacred marker, flag, or weapon unless it is separately verified for the game’s source context. A capture should be a retreat, hop, or gentle fade; never an injury animation.

## Recommended first production batch

1. **Auri and Cori:** Nine Ice Forts, Break the Ice, Sky Temple Run, and Sige.
2. **Mica:** Fishflow, Khasi Fishflow, Seven Ice Rings, Ruma Ice Puzzle, and Two Stones.
3. **Pip:** the Field guide, cover art, rules, and result pages across all twenty games.
4. Keep Four-Wing Ice Hunt, Aurora Vulture, and Ice Hunters animal-led; add the cast only in non-playable guide art.
